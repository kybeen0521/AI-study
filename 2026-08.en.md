# August 2026 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2026-08 (partial)

## 🔭 Key Trends
- **Cross-model adversarial review is emerging as the standard for coding agents.** Users repeatedly reported that flaws one model cannot catch on its own are caught by a different model (the Claude · Codex · Grok trio is a favored combination), and the prevailing sentiment is that "these days it's a verification war." That said, a counterexample (a LiveCodeBench experiment) was also shared showing that merely chaining two models does not automatically improve quality, leading to a discussion that handoff, context passing, and memory design are the real crux.
- **Role-division optimization is taking hold.** The combination of orchestration = Claude, implementation = Grok (advantage in quota and speed), and critique = Codex is converging as the best setup among many users. Codex digs relentlessly into what it is told to do but is verbose and prone to wandering, while Gemini has drawn many recent reports of degraded performance.
- **The debate over AI Scientists / autonomous research agents has intensified.** There is broad agreement that they "do well at development but cannot yet do research," that they only work in areas with established data, verification, and reproduction pipelines (mostly in silico), and that domains requiring physical validation such as clinical trials and new materials are still far off. Some suggested distinguishing between auto-research and AI4Science.
- **The "models are shared, data is the differentiator" framing** appeared repeatedly (a Texas Hold'em analogy was also shared). The Research Data Act taking effect next June (data made with taxpayer money = state-owned), the U.S. DOE's Genesis Open Models, and sovereign AI (indep-fab models) were all interpreted as being about "data pooling." There was also skepticism that the practical obstacles to data sharing (liability, copyright, lack of incentives) are large.
- **The gap between benchmarks and real-world experience** and the controversy over benchmark-specialized post-training. Some argued that human evaluation and private benchmarks are needed for fair assessment.
- **Long-horizon capability and loop performance evaluation** became a new talking point. Jeff Dean pointed to OOD (deviation from the training distribution) and "open loop" (the snowballing of small initial errors) as failure causes, and proposed "inference-time computing" as a remedy (though some found the concept vague).
- **China's low-cost, open strategy and the "shell" controversy over Korean models.** A Chinese community (Zhihu) criticized Korean models as "Qwen-based shells (套壳) and cherry-picks" (1.08 million views). Named: LG, SKT, Upstage, Motif, and Bdraft.
- **Shock over talent departures from Google.** Demis Hassabis stepping back from the front line, Jeff Dean leaving to found a startup, and so on. One backstory holds that research compute support was insufficient because TPUs were being used for services.
- **Token cost as the new bottleneck and "R&D cost."** Comments included "tokens, not GPUs, are the welfare" and the need to reflect token costs in next year's R&D budget.

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- **Research generation cost vs. verification cost**: One view held that "the cost of generating research results has fallen exponentially, but the cost of verification has stayed the same." The perspective that data is the only differentiator and that research data made with taxpayer money should rightfully be state-owned was also shared. In a review of the White House's "AI Golden Age" report, skepticism was raised about autonomous labs' verification procedures and regional clusters.
- **Practical know-how for model combinations**: A majority view formed that planning with Fable → adversarial critique with Codex is currently the best. Experiences were also shared that Codex is weak at orchestration and that its code balloons, increasing the bug surface. It was pointed out that even Claude struggles to catch flaws in its own implementation when used alone (something Claude itself acknowledges).
- **The scope of the reviewer's role**: The question was raised whether it is better for a reviewer to stay advisory only, since a reviewer who goes as far as rewriting can undermine the original author's intent. A view was also offered to reconsider the very assumption of "kibitzing" in peer review.
- **The limits of LLMs**: An analysis was shared that language is an "exceptional field where AI has succeeded" because its training distribution and usage distribution overlap well. The Yann LeCun-style point was reiterated that new insights emerge only when linguistic emergence is combined with numerical emergence, which is why a separate world model is needed. An observation was also raised that recent pretraining data is shifting from knowledge-centric to agentic and reasoning-centric data.
- **Crisis of the graduate school / lab system**: A diagnosis that "professors can do research without grad students, and students can do research without professors." Theoretical fields will be hit hard, while wet labs (chemistry, materials, bio) will be affected relatively later. There is also a "data donation" dilemma: open-sourcing tacit knowledge lets AI acquire it, eroding one's competitiveness.
- **The realism of the AI Scientist**: While hypothesis and problem discovery are useful, results must be re-checked by humans, and it was pointed out that the assumption that virtual validation matches on-site physical validation is still precarious. A perspective was shared that this differs in character from the structure of medical clinical research (the accumulation of RCTs). A pragmatic view also emerged that "in the end, whoever needs the data has to gather and use it themselves."
- **Institutional design for LLM medical AI**: Alongside an assessment that the MFDS's LLM medical device guidelines are hard to apply in the field, a view was offered proposing an institutional design that treats LLM medical AI not as a "medical device" but as a legally accountable "virtual human (a licensed person)." It was also pointed out that the public sector needs a "special task force" that performs a real role rather than a pilot, along with quality management.
- **Macro discourse**: Views such as "science feeds on war," and that U.S.-China competition is accelerating AI innovation (a Sputnik moment). The fact that solving the 10 math problems cost less than 3 million won in tokens sparked both a sense of deflation—"who would want to do research?"—and a debate over the shifting standards of knowledge work.
