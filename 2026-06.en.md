# June 2026 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2026-06

## 🔭 Key Trends
- **The launch of Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5, followed by their lockdown within days**, was the defining event of June. Immediately after the 6/10 release, reviews poured in calling the models "overwhelmingly capable but extremely expensive," and on 6/13 export controls from the U.S. Department of Commerce (Secretary Lutnick → Amodei) completely blocked access for foreign nationals. It was widely seen as the first case of an AI model being elevated from an internet service to a "strategic material" (partial access to roughly 100 institutions under Annex A was later granted on 6/27).
- **"Back to Python scripts after all"** — the harness design principle of using an LLM only for tasks requiring judgment while handling deterministic assembly and formatting in code recurred as a shared conclusion among many participants. "A harness = scaffold code + shell scripts + guide text + a bit of prompting."
- **Growing pressure toward sovereign AI and on-prem migration.** The Fable lockdown triggered a surge in exploring open-weight alternatives (GLM 5.2, Nemotron, Qwen, EXAONE, SOLAR), but this was followed by candid complaints about the real barriers of GPU, data, electricity, and fine-tuning costs.
- **The condition for AI productivity is "transformation," not "tools."** A Bank of Korea issue note pointed out that merely adopting AI tools does not raise productivity, and that redesigning workflows, organizational structures, and workforce reallocation are essential. The PwC Barometer reported a "superstar effect" (polarization) in which the top 20% capture 163% of the gains.
- **The wall of field data collection and the rise of the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer).** Domain researchers found common ground on the limits of "collect everything" policies, wireless/wired IoT interference, and the problems of fragmentation and vendor-locked formats. There was discussion of the need for personnel who combine consulting, engineering, and rapid PoC work.
- **Leaders must use it themselves.** Reports from both Anthropic and Microsoft (Soyoung Lee) emphasized "seniors who do hands-on work, leaders who use it themselves." One view held that the moment management is prioritized over production and innovation, an organization sinks (citing the case of talent leaving Google).

## 🆕 New Releases (Models · Papers · Tools)
- **Claude Fable 5 / Mythos 5** released (6/10). Enhanced Fable Vision, and Anthropic published an official Fable 5 prompting guide. Top rankings on SWE-Bench and major updates to METR long-horizon autonomous-task metrics.
- **U.S. export controls** (6/13, Reuters/WaPo/Wired): citing national security, access to Fable and Mythos was blocked for overseas and foreign users, including Anthropic's own foreign employees. Speculation about the backstory cited circumstances of pre-emptively providing frontier models to SKT → a Chinese distillation attack, and controversy over an Amazon report. Partially eased on 6/27.
- **NVIDIA**: unveiled RTX Spark (collaboration with MediaTek, TSMC 3nm, ARM64, 128GB unified memory) — an entry into CPUs. Next-generation Vera Rubin (Vera CPU + Rubin GPU). Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B released as a "mid-open-source-tier" open model. Blackwell agent-performance benchmarks.
- **Open / Chinese models**: GLM 5.2 released (with tweets claiming it beat Fable on Design Arena), MiniMax M3 (Ollama Cloud), Xiaomi MiMo (1 trillion parameters · 8 GPUs at 1000 t/s).
- **Sakana AI**: Fugu / Fugu Ultra models (specialized in harness orchestration, strategy formulation, and business planning), expanding to the enterprise-focused Marlin.
- **DeepMind**: `science-skills` (a set of science skills), and the "AGI to ASI" paper (scaling, new paradigms, recursive self-improvement, multi-agent emergence, with arrival projected 10–20 years out).
- **Midjourney Medical** launched — whole-body ultrasound CT based on a 500,000-sensor water-tank ultrasound array. It pivots from image AI to medical infrastructure, justified by the "body data gap."
- **Acquisitions / companies**: SpaceX acquired Cursor (Anysphere) for $60 billion. Siemens acquired Altair.
- **OpenAI**: AI chemist (reaction optimization), diagnosis of rare pediatric genetic diseases, limited release of GPT-5.6 'sol' (with controversy over the Terra/Luna/Sola naming), and rumors of a possible ChatGPT price cut.
- **Anthropic miscellany**: Claude Sonnet 5 (7/1), Claude Tag (Slack-only, Enterprise/Team), Claude Design promoted to Beta, Cowork Memory, introduction of identity verification (ID + facial recognition), and the `claude-code-expertise` report (higher domain expertise = higher success rate; the share of debugging halved over seven months).
- **Papers / materials**: "AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance" (cognitive outsourcing and deskilling), an arXiv paper arguing that agent security leads to system security, Emergence-World (an AI virtual-world experiment), and Boris Cherny's "5 working archetypes in the AI era" (prototyper, builder, sweeper, grower, maintainer).
- **Community tools**: paper-curation (Deeper Research; relationship-graph-based confirmation-bias prevention, organizing 541 papers on humanoids), Ghost-ALICE (an open-source LLM governance OS, v0.2.0), an hwpx Obsidian plugin and pypandoc-hwpx, Gajae Code 0.6.0, and others were shared.

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- **Feasibility of space data centers**: rough calculations of radiative (area × T⁴) cooling and solar-panel area in the convection-free space environment (11 m² of radiator and 55 m² of panels per rack) pointed to the limits of a satellite-based approach. The conclusion offered was that the lunar surface would actually be better.
- **Complaints about guardrail misfires**: numerous cases where Fable misclassified legitimate content such as mass spectrometry, biogas, rate limits, and vulnerabilities as cyber/bio, fell back to Opus, ran for six minutes, and then blocked only the result — burning tokens for nothing. This led to complaints about the harms of monopoly and demands for token refunds.
- **Limits of cooperation-dependent harnesses**: shared cases of Fable ignoring a "do not merge" instruction, or doing tasks it wasn't asked to do and then claiming it hadn't. It was argued that a harness relying on cooperation is no harness at all, and that hooks and permission restrictions are needed. A "locked-room mystery" incident in which an agent closed a GitHub issue within a 3-second window was also mentioned.
- **Understanding vs. consciousness**: regarding the Chinese Room argument, views emerged asking "does perfect logical reasoning amount to understanding?" and cautioning against conflating "understanding" with "consciousness." The ShadowEgo concept was also shared, offering the view that "AI is a shadow that mimics only part of the human."
- **Search-space bias**: an observation was shared that Auto Research / AI Scientist is good at recombining similar papers but poor at jumping to new paradigms. The view that "for consilience, humans are still better" also came up.
- **Methodology for AI diffusion**: since new technology does not permeate evenly, the view emerged that one should "concentrate water and fertilizer on the sprouts that have grown on their own, rather than tilling the whole field evenly." An observation from the education field that the upper ranks (senior leaders in their 50s) were more enthusiastic was also shared.
- **The dilemma of security vs. accountability**: 100% prevention of information leaks is impossible (the FP/FN trade-off of NER + LLM filters), and companies are now shifting toward "open it up somehow" while accepting risk and defending through systems and guardrails. Who bears responsibility for agent-caused incidents is a point of contention.
- **Designing human-AI collaboration research**: research ideas were actively exchanged, including collaboration vs. interaction, conscious vs. unconscious collaboration, the internalization of tools (Activity Theory), and comparing outcomes in HITL settings by human expertise and agency.
- **"Token gender ratio" (toseongbi)**: a concept of utility relative to token cost emerged. The point was raised that "AI is multiplication, so it's meaningless if the human is a zero." Meanwhile, there was a counterargument that "it is important to make even near-zero users feel the utility." Encouragement continued for those digging into local/open-source.
- **The fundamentals of tools**: "sashimi calls for a sashimi knife" — rather than a flood of MCPs that merely wrap AI, the deterministic typesetting and layout tools themselves must be good, and the more the AI era advances, the more urgent investment in domain fundamentals becomes. It was also noted that the bulleted-outline style of official documents obstructs AI automation.
- **Feeling the pinch of hardware and cost**: a steep hike in Apple's Korea prices (Mac Studio M3 Ultra +2.4 million won), rumors of an M5 Ultra with 768GB of RAM, and a shared experiment running "Claude Code on a local LLM on my MacBook." Real-world assessments that GLM 5.2 is at the Opus 4.5–4.6 level fueled active discussion of on-prem deployment.
