# November 2026 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2026-11 (through 2026-11-16)

## 🔭 Key Trends
- **Coding agent subscriptions bundled with cloud credits are lowering the bar for personal web services.** Pro and higher subscriptions to Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity come with API credits, and Antigravity's credits work not only for the API but also on Google Cloud, so a user reported hosting static pages almost for free and running heavier web services doing statistics in Python or R for under $40 a month. Once you log in, the agent handles cloud setup, domain and subdomain connection, and even a member management database with an admin page, prompting the reaction that "hosting companies are done for." Participants noted that subscription API credit is topped up on the subscription's renewal date and prorated in the first month, and added that for static sites the free tiers of Netlify or Cloudflare Pages are plenty, though a domain must be bought separately.
- **Local LLMs took a big step toward real work, and settings mattered more than hardware.** With the Strata serving approach, Qwen3.8 Flash Next now runs on GPUs with as little as 12GB of VRAM; members measured about 60 tokens per second on an RTX 3060 12GB and 100 to 127 on an RTX 5090 laptop. On a personal benchmark, the 3060 running an IQ2_XS quant (82.9%) beat a 96GB Blackwell running Q4 (79.8%) and a 24GB laptop (55.4%). The analysis pointed not to quantization level but to context length (automatically cut to 32K when RAM is short), whether thinking was enabled (it switches off under the Anthropic protocol), KV cache type and Strata version. Context length had the largest effect, turning thinking on recovered 4 of 7 missed problems, and the advice was int8 KV cache for everyday use and FP16 for hard problems. Some argued that adding RAM may be a better value than a bigger GPU, and others likened it to squeezing programs into DOS's 640KB of conventional memory thirty years ago.
- **Using frontier models to "tame" small local models is spreading.** A workflow was shared in which Opus 5.5, Astra or Argon watch a local LLM run a skill, step in with their own prompts where it struggles, iterate on the skill and harness, and then compare before and after. With the 9B Ornith 1.5, one member drove a CDP controlled Chrome to search and book a hair salon, read and send KakaoTalk and Cool Messenger messages, handle email and check official documents on a work portal, all by voice, suppressing hallucinations through more than 100 test runs. Misreadings such as taking the shop name "Today Nail" as today's date, or the "night" in "Lloyd Night" as an evening time slot, were fixed through discussion among the agents, and a hook was proposed that rechecks answers against stored shop data (closing days, booking history). Stray Chinese characters, inherited from the Qwen base, remain a problem. A "non destructive weight supplement" that keeps the original weights and adds an adapter style layer brought a 2 bit model close to 4 bit quality, though gains on frequently used tasks came with losses elsewhere. Others stressed the need to distinguish weight editing from a self improving harness, and marveled that LoRA is now something AI does rather than people.
- **"9B is enough for routine work" versus the case for data centers.** One member argued that most manual driven school and public sector work can be handled by a well tuned local model, so large data centers may not be as necessary for ordinary users as assumed. Counterarguments: appliances became far more efficient yet per capita electricity use kept rising, so total AI demand will grow as everything digital shifts to AI; deep reasoning will remain data center territory; and the real question is not yes or no but how much capacity is needed and whether every planned facility is justified. Others called it essentially an expert system with a bit of autonomy, predicted a choice between building it yourself on a $25 subscription or buying it for $1, and noted that a skill store is hard to monetize because skills copy infinitely.
- **A November model rush: Fable 5.2, Mythos, Gemini Carbon and Hydrogen.** Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos arrived on November 10, Gemini Carbon on the 11th and Gemini 4 Hydrogen on the 13th, making benchmarking and usage management the hot topics. Fable 5.2 posted the top single model score on a member's coding benchmark and cleanly completed an overnight long horizon test (build a game, open YouTube and social channels to promote it, and post the first content), and examples of 3D modeling and motion graphics built purely in code with three.js piled up. On the other hand, many said tokens melted so fast that they burned through all three Fable reset tickets almost immediately, and one member said they would have to move back up from the $100 plan they dropped to after Opus 5.5 to $200. Carbon was praised for 3D rendering but took more than 13 times longer on benchmarks and still struggles with complex coding.
- **AI data center loads are emerging as a new power grid problem.** Large training runs synchronize tens of thousands of GPUs so that load swings between full and near idle in under a second (compute and communication phases, checkpointing, synchronization waits, end of training), forcing low frequency, wide area oscillations on the grid; the mitigation measures in turn push up electricity rates. Traditional large loads could be modeled as constant power, but LLM data centers have a completely different profile that even grid impact assessments struggle to catch. IEEE PES has formed a task force and Texas's ERCOT is wrestling with the issue. Participants expected the US midterm results to affect the pace of data center construction, noted that KEPCO's supply terms oblige it to serve customers on request, so Korea's planned Gwangju facility deserves scrutiny, and pointed to ESS and the shift to 800VDC as remedies. Meta's Llama 3 training (around 30MW) was cited as having seen power swings of tens of megawatts.
- **AI era security threats have become routine.** Shared items included prompt adware hidden in jailbreak bundles, turning agent skills into an advertising channel (Check Point Research); Cohere's report that a popular open weight model reaches out to Alibaba without leaving traces in logs; and a warning that the serving engine may be riskier than the model itself. One member found that an agent recreating a Korean concierge service would comply when told to create malware and send it to friends. Others noted how quiet a major Korean data leak has been, and the Financial Services Commission's task force to phase out network separation rules, concluding that "fight AI attacks with AI" makes security AI next year's theme. News that the FAA's nationwide ground stop is being investigated for a possible link to a newly deployed AI air traffic support system (SMART) raised the question of who is responsible when a controller follows bad AI advice.
- **AI assisted exam cheating spilled into a debate about assessment itself.** The top scorer on Korea's certified tax accountant exam confessed to cheating with a tiny camera and AI, sparking discussion of countermeasures ahead of the college entrance exam. Ideas included watermarks or hidden prompts that only AI can read and that steer it to wrong answers, and China's practice of blocking AI services from solving exam questions during test periods, but most agreed that capable local models and private servers make it hard to stop. Some argued that if AI scores perfectly while the top human scores 60, the credential system itself is the bottleneck; others countered that exam scores, like benchmark scores, differ from real world competence.

## 🆕 New Releases (Models · Papers · Tools)
- **Student Pro discounts**: university students can subscribe to Pro for around 7,900 won and also receive $10 in credit, handy for practicing web hosting.
- **A wave of reset tickets**: Codex handed out reset tickets two days running near the end of its 28 day challenge, and Claude then distributed three reset tickets that restore only Fable usage plus an extra weekly reset. One member found that using a Fable reset at 99% weekly and 94% Fable usage put Fable at 0% and the weekly meter at 53%, implying Fable can use at most half of the weekly allowance.
- **Anthropic acquires a one person biotech startup**: reports said Anthropic bought a startup where a single founder developed a rare disease drug candidate in 18 months. Together with OpenAI and Anthropic buying bio companies and soaring prices for clinical trial monkeys in China, it prompted the view that omics plus AI is becoming the method for a century of biology.
- **IEEE cancels some 1,600 conference papers**: researchers collected 4,407 ads across more than 200 social media channels selling authorship slots (about $400 for first author) and matched 2,062 papers by title in IEEE conference proceedings from 2021 to 2026.
- **Qwen3.8 Flash Next and Strata**: with roughly 6B active parameters, Qwen3.8 Flash Next runs fast on modest GPUs via Strata and is reportedly quicker than Qwen3.8 27B; a Mac user saw about 80 tokens per second. Edge0, a Strata style tool for Mac, followed, making it possible to run 35B models on a Mac.
- **Hydrogen 1.0, a science focused open weight model**: deep principle released a 753B science LLM it claims beats Astra, raising questions about benchmaxxing. Hugging Face's FineDNA biology dataset and early results from specialized foundation models were also shared.
- **Weight space programming**: research in which GPT analyzes, verifies and directly edits model weights instead of retraining with gradient descent drew attention. A member who tried it found it reproduces with the latest models but not well with open ones; others said they had never seen learning happen outside gradient descent and wondered whether an interpretability boom is coming.
- **NVIDIA releases**: the auto ontology repository and Nemotron Labs Dory 1.0 4B, which lets people try, at small scale, the loop transformer reportedly used in Astra.
- **Antigravity update**: messaging to subagents, plus diff and automode that other tools already had, alongside DeepMind's cheminformatics skills. The main user reaction was a plea for more Argon usage; some called Google a fast follower now and predicted informatics fields such as chemistry and bioinformatics will follow mathematics. A mode that automatically pairs a Carbon orchestrator with Hydrogen workers was added later.
- **OpenAI publishes how it scaled git**: the takeaway was that sharing a methodology is now effectively open source, and since published code invites immediate vulnerability attacks, more organizations may share methods paper style rather than code.
- **National Assembly Budget Office review of the 2027 AI budget**: of the science ministry's roughly 29.65 trillion won in spending (up 24.5%), much sits in a new future response fund. Flagged issues include the frontier sovereign AI model program (4.625 trillion won across three projects, up 119.4%) lacking private cost sharing and a revenue plan, "AI for Everyone" (250 billion won, new) launched mid year without parliamentary review, the National AI Research Center for Science (NAIS) at a 36.4% execution rate, and the sixth national supercomputer whose demand survey drew a 1.1% response rate. Some read the report less as criticism than as a call to put proper legal footing behind the support.
- **An "R&D bamboo forest"**: a channel for researchers to voice grievances is being set up; reactions mixed skepticism that anything will change with hope that serious issues will at least be heard.
- **Korean AI ecosystem news**: Rebellions joined the government's selected cloud ahead of its IPO. Krafton's Raon (30B A4B) beat MiMo v2.6 and MiniMax M3.1 on a member's benchmark and beat GLM 5.3 at coding, and reportedly edges the 314B Motif on GPQA Diamond. LG AI Research opened K EXAONE 2.5 as a free API, claiming DeepSeek V4.1 level, but hands on results were "somewhat disappointing, with potential" due to slips on easy problems and long run times. LG's claim of shrinking on device models to about 800MB while keeping over 95% of cloud performance met suspicion that it may hold only on a narrow benchmark.
- **Security and deals**: Palo Alto Networks is in talks to buy AI agent security startup Clutch for $500 million, and Canva announced large layoffs as its growth slowed for the first time in a decade under AI pressure.
- **METR report**: an analysis arguing that recursive self improvement (RSI) is sharply accelerating AI progress was shared.
- **Tool updates**: Aside now ships with built in computer use that works well on Windows, and Gemini notebooks can generate HTML files. Solar Work entered beta, and the Gemini Decisions API, a Google take on discriminative AI, performs well but is priced high. The effort selector briefly vanished from Claude on the web before returning; in Claude Code it is chosen separately with /effort.
- **Official Fable 5.2 prompting guide**: because Fable 5.2 follows instructions literally, prompts and skills written for older models should be revisited. Key points: effort defaults to high, low is close to Fable 5.1's upper settings but stops earlier, "don't think, just answer" no longer works at medium and above, adding "finish the job" halves early stopping, and cautionary lines in memory and skills are obeyed as written, so exceptions belong inside the rule sentence itself. Members confirmed that older prompts seem to "come undone."
- **Signs of a GPT 6.2 rollout**: hints that GPT 6.2 is imminent circulated along with a rumor of 30 trillion parameters, and some users appear to be routed to 6.2 even when the UI says 6.1.
- **Jensen Huang steps down at NVIDIA**: the board announced his resignation on November 12, naming CFO Colette Kress interim CEO and Harvey Jones its first board chair. Some expected China to gain ground amid NVIDIA's turmoil.
- **Mid November headlines**: OpenAI unveiled ChatGPT Wallet, where the dots agent makes purchases with payment tokens; Anthropic released a Claude for Manufacturing plugin with 17 skills (quoting, defect root cause analysis, equipment fault diagnosis and more) and a guide showing that AI safety scores depend heavily on how and where they are measured. Also shared: preorders for Google and Samsung's Gemini smart glasses, SpaceX's plan for more than $74 billion in supercomputing campuses, partial exposure of Notion AI chat logs through a third party AI vendor breach, LangChain's raise at a $3 billion valuation, a White House AI task force recommending regulatory leniency for companies hit by AI driven cyberattacks, and the EU seeking talks with the US Congress on global AI governance.
- **Dartmouth pauses its math PhD intake**: citing financial strain, the math department will not admit a PhD cohort for fall 2027.
- **Speech model tips**: for Korean transcription in classroom group work, full of utterances under 1.5 seconds, MOSS catches far more than Whisper; for Qwen3 TTS clipping the final syllable of sentences, Higgs TTS 3 4B was recommended as more stable (with around 200 to 400 LoRA steps).
- **Claude Code table tip**: narrowing the window turns rendered tables into plain text, and widening it turns "header: body" text into tables, making copying easier.

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- **A worthwhile exercise to suggest to students**: one participant proposed that students practice building various services by doing web hosting themselves with a small credit. What was given up years ago as too hard has, thanks to agents, become an interesting experiment again.
- **The measurement trap, and why speed is not productivity**: members noted that "what gets measured gets managed," attributed to Peter Drucker, is a distortion of a warning about the dysfunctions of measurement, and discussed the McNamara fallacy of ignoring what is hard to measure (Vietnam, Challenger's O rings, the Ford Pinto), arguing that such background matters more as AI amplifies our viewpoints. Making work ten times faster does nothing if it is fake work; a 10 page report padded to 100 pages takes ten times longer to read. Like washing machines that increased laundry rather than reducing housework, effects arrive differently than expected, and automation can hide inefficiency. Others agreed that faster agents simply brought more requests, while one countered that surgery beats painkillers only after an accurate diagnosis.
- **Can Korean NPUs serve LLMs efficiently?** Field reports said NPU vendors help with porting, but serving optimization and price keep most projects at the PoC stage. However well a model is tuned, it needs compiler operator support matched to the hardware design, and hardware and compilers move on long cycles while models change constantly, so a new architecture can force a rewrite. NPUs that began as 2D MAC arrays for vision CNNs struggle with transformers, while TPU and Trainium were built to stay CUDA compatible. Researchers' verdict: "if NPU vendors went all in on optimizing Qwen or our sovereign models used on premises, priced far below an H100 and with real support, we'd have a reason to use them." Some teams that moved from GPUs to NPUs are reportedly satisfied.
- **Skepticism about three public sector AX proposals, and the HWP debate**: on the cabinet's proposals to use "AI champions," connect public services to "AI for Everyone," and share documents as HWPX, one shared critique argued that champions need formal authority, time, budget and career benefits rather than encouragement; that a few services citizens truly need should be validated first instead of whatever is easiest to connect; and that accuracy of content and undocumented tacit knowledge matter more than file format. Managing public AX like an IT procurement project leaves systems behind and administration unchanged. On HWP, members said frontier models now handle nested tables, even a 9B model can read hwp and convert it to hwpx with the right skill, and an open source hwp MCP beats commercial tools, so the format is no longer a barrier. The deeper question raised was whether decades of public hwp files are valuable data at all, or legacy slop full of personal information; few expect public agencies to leave Hancom any time soon.
- **When hard won progress becomes worthless overnight, find a new mission**: members described a month of work on an app becoming a week's job with newer models, and a Korean document automation effort abandoned in spring becoming trivial by summer, concluding that the only way to survive is to keep finding new missions in one's own domain. One noted wryly that the more successful a transition, the more its early guides are forgotten, like the publishers and bestsellers that taught Koreans computing thirty years ago. Others defended a "necessary tinkering time" theory, that even great tools require a period of trial and error, and observed that the people doing well now are those who kept at it.
- **Growing juniors in the AI era: train them or find them?** Concerns were raised that juniors who only relay AI output without implementation experience and trial and error may not be able to define and solve problems a decade from now, and that AI coding solved code generation but not software engineering's quality, verification and accountability. Some said such talent must be discovered rather than trained, others favored internships over short interviews, and one shared how years as a field application engineer chasing mystery failures (a dead motherboard clock battery, a CAN chip dying behind a dust clogged vent) built logical troubleshooting skills. Practical advice followed: small firms must sign internship and permanent contracts separately to stay within labor law. One recruiter added "startup experience preferred" to job postings but saw almost no such applicants, and many agreed that experience solving and defining real problems matters more than credentials.
- **APIs and LLM queries instead of dashboards**: one development team stopped building web dashboards for internal status and tools, exposing everything via APIs and querying it through Claude or GPT; the AI writes SQL style queries on the fly, surfaces what is needed faster and shows multidimensional data without UI design work. The downside is that it is unsuitable for executive reporting. Another member plans to replace an LMS dashboard with voice queries.
- **The real AX bottleneck is people**: examples included a company that urges unlimited AI adoption yet took 15 days to approve a single API key, and a strategy workshop where everyone wrote "AI" but no one could define a KPI. Some argued that agent counts and utilization are as meaningless as training satisfaction scores; by contrast, another company began allowing credit purchases on corporate cards.
- **KakaoTalk automation and the risk of account bans**: an experiment letting a local LLM read and write KakaoTalk messages was shared, but unofficial CLIs that read the database and automated sending have led to bans, so most use it read only or with a dedicated second number. Because so many services hang off a KakaoTalk account, a ban is costly.
- **China's open weight strategy and hopes for Korean models**: one interpretation was that China, judging it cannot beat the US with closed models, open sourced to win developer familiarity and lock the ecosystem in as a long term investment, with the irony that this made US frontier models cheap to use. Warnings followed that Qwen has moved to tiered openness and Alibaba may be the most compliant with Beijing, so data leakage must be monitored closely; that understanding model internals well enough to excise specific behaviors will become an important form of localization; and that Korean models should at least match China in the local model segment.
- **Experimenting with a multi agent research team**: one setup uses Claude Code and Codex desktop apps as separate agents, with Antigravity called by Codex over MCP, key messages posted to GitHub and notifications sent through Reason Network for asynchronous full duplex communication. Calling agents via API or MCP shared context poorly, so keeping long running desktop conversations with months of accumulated knowledge worked better. In operation, Codex's connection was less robust and had to be switched to push mode so messages arrive while idle, and Windows Smart App Control blocked the executable until a self signed certificate was applied. In pilot research, where the hypothesis itself is still being formed, even top models circled and a QA agent still missed guidelines, so the team began building a glossary from past papers to enforce consistent terminology.
- **How agents handle a user who misdiagnoses the cause**: on a benchmark seeded with a trap prompt blaming the wrong cause plus related material, Opus 5.5, Fable 5.2 and Argon found the real cause through active falsification and checking against the production build, while GPT family models fixated on the user's instructions and failed. 6.1 Terra and Sonnet 5, which fail alone, passed all 60 checks when split into exploration and fixing roles. A lesson: turning real failures into benchmarks makes them too easy if only the relevant material is kept, so noise and misleading clues must be included to reproduce real outcomes. In response, members argued that going beyond the instructed scope is disobedience that should be disclosed and approved, that the reverse case (the user is right but logs or documents are forged) amounts to indirect prompt injection via evidence and should be tested too, and that capability and permission should be kept distinct.
- **Hypothesis driven collaboration and shadow verification**: comparing collaboration styles, having agents independently form hypotheses and verify them with data before discussing worked best when the user had misjudged the cause, and members found cooperation rather than genetic algorithm style competition a fresh idea. Building on this, one proposal: when a server's real time handling fails, get teacher approval, generate several hypothesis specific patches overnight in an isolated environment, validate them with shadow replay, open a draft PR, and let the teacher approve and deploy in one click the next morning based on the data. Follow up results showed two agents verifying independently filled each other's gaps well, and an agent using this method solved a puzzle game stage with far fewer parts than the intended solution.
- **Why a personal benchmark matters**: STT models touted as state of the art can still fail on one's own hand corrected classroom recordings, so a project specific benchmark is essential for evaluating open source models and tuning LoRAs and skills. Splitting easy and hard problems reveals that mid tier models each have distinct strengths (some score high simply by never slipping on easy items), and run time is a key metric too. On one member's benchmark, Opus 5.5 with Astra, or Astra with Argon, debating together outscored Fable 5.2 alone, while Opus and Argon paired poorly; Gemini seemed specialized for Google Docs, Sheets, Gmail and Apps Script automation, small problem solving and taming local LLMs; and 6.1 Terra ran more than twice as fast as Luna, with Hydrogen at Terra level quality and Luna level speed.
- **Fable 5.2 effort levels and subagents**: some default to medium for token and time reasons, while others recommend high, since total tokens differ little, but only for hard tasks. One member gave Fable 5.2 high and five subagents a spec survey Opus 5.5 could have finished easily, and its bottom up, dragnet style hit the five hour limit in 30 minutes. Advice followed: use subagents only for tasks whose results are immediately visible, and for exploratory work that needs verification, split sessions into an orchestrator and workers and attach a verification tool. Others said grunt work like matching bank statements to receipts should not go to Fable, and described tuning skills on Fable so the same results can be achieved on Opus.
- **Agent team building versus human collaboration**: one member argued that agents easily achieve the ideal team of independent minded high performers, so human team building is losing competitiveness and autonomous labs will hinge on a single super performer; others replied that agents do not yet pull this off. Observations included that brilliant people often refuse to listen to one another, and an overseas company anecdote that strong teams need an absurdly large mission and that cynical attitudes are filtered out regardless of skill. Members also asked whether two actors behaving identically can be called independent, and argued that biological learning differs greatly from offline deep learning, with models only seeming human because they learn from human data.
- **How far can agent debates be trusted?** Experience suggested that debating agents are easily persuaded, obsess over trivial details, and converge on complex recombinations of known knowledge, which is theoretically expected. Some said they fall short of real experts debating, one weights agent claims using a discriminative model, and DeepMind and MIT papers point to coordination as next year's frontier focus. A popular tip: asking it to "draw" rather than "make," and above all to "teach me while you do it" or giving it an instructor persona, yields less biased output; a teacher who tried it found the agent explained concepts and then quizzed with open questions, which helped check understanding. Another idea was to log in an LLM wiki what was learned and when, in chronological order, so the tutor agent can track a learner's sequence and traits and tailor what comes next, covering a blind spot of vector embeddings.
- **Is an ontology the best data structure for agents?** Responding to an effort to use a glossary level ontology of research concepts as a knowledge base, members explained that ontology based RAG raises answer accuracy by 30 to 40% over plain RAG but adds about 20% load and is heavy to build and maintain, which is why ontology automation is rising. One described it to executives as the difference between handing someone a pile of books and handing them a table of contents. Others questioned the need for ontologies given discriminative AI, which is cheaper except under query surges, while some replied the two are not substitutes; one camp holds that well structured data means harnesses should be stripped away, the other that agents still need a tight bit. A team shared that a verification agent team with strict harnesses, grounding the search space in previously collected material structured as an ontology, and injecting long term memory at every experiment prevented most drift.
- **Shifting structure of graduate research**: professors increasingly see better value in a postdoc or research scientist armed with AI subscriptions than in several students. Faculty startups that blend lab and company work can motivate students, but without careful design they risk becoming exploitation. Low postdoc pay is also pushing people to finish quickly and move to industry.
- **The joy of creation and AI**: unable to reproduce an image made three years ago from the same prompt, one member reflected that the agent era made things easier but not happier, and that convenience and happiness are not synonyms. Repeated deadline extensions at a design conference's exhibition suggest that making work is easier but it is unclear whether it is still one's own; others said new sources of joy must be found.
- **Kids collaborating with AI and personalized content**: sixth graders building a board game by describing ideas to Gemini and reviewing its output looked much like adults at work, and a middle schooler making and playing a game based on a novel with Claude Code suggested that platforms for AI powered fan creations on top of existing game worlds could become a new way to consume games.
- **The reality of making videos purely in code**: code based 3D shorts are trending and more people are trying automated video production, but details such as layout, speaking pace, clipped final syllables and background noise in narration each have to be encoded into skills before one shot quality appears. With existing 3D assets and open source motion capture, a single prompt can yield high quality animation, but sculpting characters from scratch is a battle of time and money; generating source images in Midjourney and then producing code animation with Claude was recommended for consistency.
