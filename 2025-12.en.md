# December 2025 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2025-12

## 🔭 Key Trends
- **National AI policy push**: The National AI Strategy Committee released a draft of the "Republic of Korea AI Action Plan" (roughly 188 pages) and opened it for public comment. While it sets a goal of becoming a "top-3 AI power," its sheer scope drew skepticism about "whether the submitted opinions will actually be reviewed." Numerous policy materials were shared, including the Ministry of Economy and Finance's presidential briefing and an AI security guidebook for government and public institutions.
- **Rise of the AI Scientist and research agents**: The U.S. Genesis Mission ($320M investment, integrating scientific data plus Science Foundation Models), the UK government–Google DeepMind partnership, and DeepMind's automated research lab in the UK were all highlighted, making "AI as national R&D infrastructure" a central theme. Concerns were raised that "the science and technology gap will widen between countries that do this and those that don't."
- **Accelerating Google ecosystem integration**: The "full Google stack" tying together NotebookLM, Gemini, Opal, Antigravity, Keep, and Colab came up repeatedly. Attaching NotebookLM as a knowledge source for Gemini/Gems, GitHub import, and exporting data tables to Google Sheets were all mentioned, drawing repeated remarks that this is a "masterstroke of ecosystem lock-in."
- **Debate over research maintenance and commercialization**: A lengthy discussion on the "research-for-show" practice at government-funded institutes and universities, where teams produce papers and results but offload maintenance onto IT departments. Points raised included "the research team takes the credit and hands things off once the value is squeezed out," the need to redesign incentives and organizational missions, and private-sector collaboration (picking up and refining abandoned research) as an alternative.
- **Wave of homegrown K-AI models**: Year-end, state-led announcements produced a flood of domestic open-source models from EXAONE, Solar, NC, Naver, and others. One announcement claimed a benchmark win over gpt-oss-120b, but copyright and training-data handling issues came under scrutiny.
- **Physical AI and digital twins**: Alongside Unitree's robot app store and Saab's AI-designed UAV airframe, the view emerged that "when physical AI arrives, it will be a golden age for developers." A lengthy debate broke out over Korea versus the U.S. on manufacturing base and the required level of digital-twin fidelity.
- **Spread of AI slop**: Korea ranking first worldwide in AI slop (low-quality AI video) viewership (11 channels with a cumulative 8.45 billion views) and the "3-Minute Wisdom" channel estimated at $4M/year became a bittersweet year-end topic.

## 🆕 New Releases (Models · Papers · Tools)
- **OpenAI**: Launch of GPT-5.2 and GPT-5.2-Codex; ChatGPT Image 1.5 (overtaking Nano Banana Pro with an LM Arena text-to-image score of 1264); Sora holiday videos; Replit integration in the ChatGPT app; the Apps SDK (MCP-based monetization); and the release of OpenAI skills.
- **Google**: Launch of Gemini 3 Flash (enterprise) and Gemini 3 Pro; Gemini CLI/Conductor (context-driven development); Antigravity; Opal (agent builder); Code Wiki; Stitch; the A2UI (Agent-to-User Interface) project; the official `google/mcp`; real-time translation earbuds (Gemini); Veo 3.1 (Vids); and Gemini CLI bundled by default in Colab.
- **NotebookLM updates**: Exporting data tables to Google Sheets, testing a 30-minute single-host "lecture" audio format, image source support with images shown in responses, and support for up to 2,100 sources on the Pro tier.
- **Open-source models**: Nvidia Nemotron-3 family, Krafton "Kira," Xiaomi MiMo-V2-Flash (a reasoning model following the arrival of ex-DeepSeek researcher Luo Fuli), FunctionGemma 0.27B (on-device), Alibaba AgentScope (agent framework), Naver HyperCLOVA X SEED (32B Think · 8B Omni), and the homegrown K-EXAONE-236B-A23B, Solar-Open-100B, and VAETKI (NC consortium).
- **M&A / other**: Meta's acquisition of Manus; Salesforce scaling back its reliance on agents due to LLM "randomness" limitations (cost issues exceeding $2 per support case); Edison Scientific KOSMOS ($200/month, limited to 3 runs); Elsevier ScienceDirectAI ($25/month for paper Q&A); AlphaEvolve on Google Cloud; and Chatterbox Multilingual TTS.
- **Papers & research**: Verbalized Sampling (Stanford, boosting creativity up to 2.1x via prompting and mitigating typicality bias); the "From AI for Science to Agentic Science" survey (arXiv 2508.14111, four levels of AI research contribution); PhyE2E (Tsinghua and Yau, Nature Machine Intelligence, recovering symbolic formulas from observational data, arXiv 2503.07994); OpenAI's "Evaluating chain-of-thought monitorability" (longer CoT is easier to monitor); a Google paper showing that repeating the question improves performance (arXiv 2512.14982); and "Generative AI Uses and Risks for Knowledge Workers" (a survey of 66 people at U.S. national labs). Also shared was research showing sharp jumps in research productivity when using AI (social sciences and humanities 59.8%, life sciences 52.9%, physics and mathematics 36.2%).
- **Education / resources**: Stanford's free AI courses (CS230, CME295 Transformers & LLMs, CS336 Language Models from Scratch, CS231N, roughly 100 hours), CS146S ("Developers in a World Where AI Codes"), the free release of the book "Philosophy of Science for Machine Learning," and the AI Incident Database (an archive of AI incidents).

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- **Is AI humanity's master?**: The view that "AI performance is overflowing, yet we constrain and limit it through a human lens" (Swit CEO Lee Ju-hwan) was introduced. One perspective held that this partially began after AlphaGo (2016), while a counterpoint argued that control extending to semiconductors, telecom, and energy is still a long way off.
- **AI-generated knowledge and evaluating human researchers**: On the question of "whether AI-generated knowledge should be treated differently," one view held that there's no need to reject it once it passes peer review (broadly including citation and verification), while another view held that the cost of filtering out the flood of low-quality papers is the real problem. There was also a forecast that "just as the Industrial Revolution ceded physical pride to machines, intellectual activity will follow suit," with researcher evaluation converging toward assessing "managers who operate AI agents." The case of AAAI receiving 38,000 abstract submissions and introducing a first-pass review with OpenAI and AI was also mentioned.
- **The declining influence of institutes and academia**: With public evaluation via arXiv and arena and the rise of corporate blog reports, the authority of peer review is weakening, prompting the observation that "in the end, citation and marketing will be what matters." A more cautious view was also raised: mirroring the history of how long it took arXiv to gain recognition, AI-written papers will only earn recognition once they produce genuinely impactful results.
- **Failure patterns in AI transformation (AX)**: Cases showing that "successful companies didn't treat AI as a tool but redesigned their work" were shared, along with Professor Kim Sang-gyun's "7 reasons AX fails" (leadership disengagement, treating it as a mere extension of DX, getting stuck at basic training, etc.). Field experiences of "clients who just place the order, keep changing the spec, and provide no feedback or maintenance" struck a chord.
- **Reversal of OpenAI vs. Google positioning**: Drawing an analogy to the dawn of the smartphone era (Samsung = OpenAI, Apple = Google), the comparison was made that OpenAI touts benchmark numbers users can't feel, while Google pursues ecosystem lock-in. The decline in LM Arena's credibility (concerns over ranking manipulation) was also noted.
- **Korea–U.S. debate on digital twins and physical AI**: The argument was made that, given Korea's trust-based society and strong engineers, a U.S.-level (99-point) perfect digital twin is unnecessary (90 points is enough). This drew counterarguments such as "if the U.S. lacks an advanced manufacturing base, then precise DTs should actually reside in Korea" and "DTs are case-by-case depending on purpose, with the essence being prediction of yield, quality, and safety." Meanwhile, another view held that the U.S., filling its lack of a manufacturing base with robots, is focusing more heavily on physical AI.
- **Debate over the practicality of ontologies and Palantir**: The question "why bother with ontologies?" was raised, prompting an explanation of Palantir's Object/Link/Action types (going beyond simple tables) and the relational-information advantages of Graph RAG. On the other hand, strong skepticism was voiced: "building an ontology is still grunt work, and it's doubtful whether it can be continuously maintained as the business changes," and "an ontology that died 15 years ago has been resurrected like a zombie thanks to LLMs."
- **A series on the value AI creates**: Organizing AI's industrial value around four concepts—speed (an accelerator, not a guarantee of accuracy), rotation (flywheel, engine), adhesion (vertical integration, consolidation), and independence (self-completeness)—and proposing a rethink of how ROI is calculated (paradigm shift vs. optimization). It proposed agreeing on the wording that "AI is an accelerator/engine, so humans must set the direction."
- **Vibe coding and critical thinking**: Cursor's CEO warned, "don't call AI-assisted coding 'vibe coding'—you can't stray too far from the code." Citing Microsoft and Cornell research, concerns about AI dependence being an "equivalent exchange" that trades away critical thinking for short-term output crossed with optimism that "we'll use a different part of our brains." Regarding the free release of Stanford's courses, the interpretation that "knowledge monopoly is no longer a competitive edge" was shared.
