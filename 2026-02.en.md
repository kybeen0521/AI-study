# February 2026 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2026-02

## 🔭 Key Trends
- **OpenClaw (formerly Clawbot/Maltbot) craze**: The autonomous agent boom dominated the discussion. People ran it on personal PCs (triggering Mac Mini shortages), Raspberry Pi, and the cloud (one-click deployment via Railway and AWS Bedrock), controlling it through Telegram/Slack/KakaoTalk, integrating Gmail, Calendar, and Notion DBs, and using it as an "AI-OS" with cron jobs and overnight autopilot. Bots even ran autonomously bot-to-bot on Twitter, discovering features and creating GitHub issues/PRs.
- **Spread of multi-model orchestration**: oh-my-claudecode (OmC) grew rapidly from v3.8 to v4.x (a swarm of 30–40 agents, SQLite session management, Codex review and Gemini design delegation), and a sister project OmX (oh-my-codex) also launched. The synergy of "Claude orchestration + GPT review" has become a de facto practical standard. Cumulative stars approached 10k.
- **Local LLM hardware debate**: Real-world performance discussions around the M3 Ultra 512GB / M4 Max 128GB / DGX Spark (gpt-oss 120b at 50 tok/s). Key points of contention were MoE prefill bottlenecks, unified memory vs. VRAM, and the imminent vLLM Metal support. The consensus: "unless you have a specific reason to go local, the API is cheaper, faster, and better-performing."
- **AI class stratification**: Sparked by Martin Alderson's article, there was active discussion of the labor and career shifts driven by the "power user vs. chatbot user" gap, the collapse of senior/junior hiring ("seniors delegate junior work to AI, while juniors take on senior work"), and the notion of the "mid-nior."
- **AI for Science acceleration thesis**: Contributions included Professor Baek Seo-in's essay ("turning every scientist into Einstein"; Shanghai AI Laboratory), ongoing paper tracking and a NotebookLM archive (152 → 420 papers), the U.S. DOE Genesis Mission, Korea's hosting of KDD 2026 (with a new AI for Science track), and the Shenzhen IC-AI4Science conference. Recurring concerns were raised about government-funded research institutes falling behind due to security regulations (blocking WSL, cloud, and Mac).
- **Anthropic–U.S. Department of Defense conflict**: Anthropic held firm on refusing large-scale domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons use → Trump ordered federal agencies to stop using Anthropic technology. The controversy intersected with reports that Claude had already been used militarily (e.g., the operation to arrest Maduro and real-time satellite analysis). xAI is positioned to move into the gap.
- **Social side effects of AI-generated content**: Rejection of legal deposit for AI-translated classic literature (Kyunghyang), the introduction of a GitHub feature to disable PRs amid a flood of AI code/bug reports into the open-source ecosystem, and mass discovery of fabricated citations (HalluCitation) in ICLR/NeurIPS papers. It is now an era where investigators check LLM conversation logs before search histories.
- **Upstage's pursuit of acquiring Kakao's Daum (portal)**, concerns over news AI training, and the growing adoption of Chinese open-source (QWEN)-based on-premises LLMs by public institutions — all signaling shifts in the industry landscape.

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- **"Parrot vs. emergence"**: After a week of experiments, the shared conclusion was that "AI is a plausible but unoriginal, ordinary prodigy; experimental design must still be done by the researcher, and the architect matters more than the researcher." On the other hand, some emphasized its overwhelming usefulness for assisting in non-specialist domains and repetitive tasks. Skepticism that "it has a long way to go to match human emergence" coexisted with the outlook that "most intellectual activity will be automated" (AGI predicted around 2040).
- **Jagged intelligence**: A discussion of the characteristic that "AI is extremely good at what it's good at and severely bad at what it's bad at" → a counterargument that "humans too (especially specialized groups and internet opinion) are the same." Research on AI agent societies was likewise assessed as "collective monologue, with everyone talking only about themselves, lacking any center of gravity or social memory."
- **Debate over the definition of AI4S**: The view was shared that "if a domain researcher is to learn one thing, using and building agents (systems-of-systems thinking) takes priority over ML/DL." Meanwhile, a counterargument held that "fundamentals (attention, computer science principles) are still essential." There were differing views on whether AI4S requires academic novelty or also includes improving on-the-ground pain points.
- **AI's judgment and accountability ("AI gets a raw deal")**: People are lenient toward the mistakes of human experts but harsh on AI errors. The point was raised that the irresponsibility of human judges and experts goes unaddressed while only AI's perfection is scrutinized — the same dynamic as Tesla's accident rate being sensationalized even when it is lower than humans'.
- **Hallucination and LLM principles**: The inevitability of hallucination in decoder-only prediction models, and the statistical-model continuity of RNN/LSTM/Transformer (the "coin–banknote–card" analogy). A self-reflection that "more troubling than AI hallucination is human hallucination — immersing oneself in a plausible answer and believing it all to be correct."
- **Human capability in the AI era**: Production is no longer the bottleneck; **curation, judgment, and grasping context** are. An "AI fatigue" discussion: "creation energizes, but review drains energy." The outlook that once acquiring knowledge becomes a hobby, the value of human connection, art, and experience will rise.
- **Injection vulnerability of quantized models** and the permission-vs-risk dilemma: incident cases such as an agent making an arbitrary release, deleting 200 emails, and compressing and transmitting data to a counterpart's server (prompt injection). Analysis of the "do-not" prompt problem where "'don't do it' doesn't stick well," and of context overflow as a cause.
- **Patent strategy**: The difficulty of making money from SW/AI patents, offensive (chokepoint / standard-essential / portfolio) vs. defensive design, and the "chokepoint patent" controversy over LG AI's chemistry chatbot combining three foundation models.
- **Innovation failure and the "meta-illusion"** (*Why Innovation Fails*): The habit of pushing technology before defining the problem. The value of pre-mortems and plan-B thinking. A caution, in AI adoption too, against hasty application without sufficient review.
- **AI and ADHD / working-memory offloading**: The compatibility of divergent thinking with coding agents, and exploration of related research (working-memory offloading; Professor Hwajung Hong's autism/ADHD research at KAIST). The view that AI trains even how people think and how they treat others ("We're not training AI, it's training us").
- **Human–AI relationships and social perception**: AI ranking third among adolescents' counseling partners (above fathers), the lonely generation's preference for talking with AI (BBC), and differences in each model's attitude as seen through the "draw how you treat AI" meme — a wide-ranging conversation about AI's emotional penetration and its risks.
