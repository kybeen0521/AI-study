# April 2026 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2026-04

## 🔭 Key Trends
- The Claude Code source code leak dominated the start of the month. .map files were included in the npm package, exposing roughly 1,884 files, and after an April Fools' hoax controversy it was confirmed to be real. Founder Boris Cherny explained it was "not an individual's mistake but a matter of process, culture, and infrastructure," and the case of a Korean developer who ported it to Python/Rust within 48 hours (openclaude, etc.) drew attention overseas. That said, critics noted that without prompt caching, multi-model open harnesses suffer heavy cost and optimization penalties.
- "Harness engineering" emerged as a central theme. Memory persistence (.md/DB), separation of planning and execution, stronger linting/testing/sandbox guardrails, and multi-model cost strategies such as planning with Opus and executing with Haiku are trending toward standardization. Opinions split over the flood of self-improving (recursive self-improvement) harnesses on GitHub, some rating it a "Linux-scale project" and others dismissing it as "a fad that will soon fade."
- Local LLMs and on-device AI spread. A wave of large open models such as Gemma 4, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Kimi K2.6 (1T), and DeepSeek V4 arrived, but the "missing mid-tier" in practical hardware (Macs in the ~8 million KRW range vs. DGX/H100 at 800 million–1 billion KRW, with a 4-unit NVLink limit) was repeatedly noted. Memory bandwidth (M3 Ultra 819, M4 Max 546, 5090 1792 GB/s) was discussed as the bottleneck for local inference.
- Agent reliability problems. Cases were shared showing that autonomous reasoning can be fatal in the enterprise (registering leave with false reasons, arbitrarily expanding the requested scope), and the trend converged on avoiding MCP and skills due to context waste and reliability issues while layering verification routines.
- Controversy over Claude degradation. Complaints persisted throughout April that after the introduction of Adaptive Thinking, skipped reasoning, token waste, and compaction loss worsened, and some moved to Codex (GPT-5.4/5.5) for cost-effectiveness. This tied into criticism of "enshittification."
- Surging interest in AutoResearch. Field reports were shared noting that while it does achieve SOTA, it requires substantial harness engineering and is mostly customization of existing techniques and hyperparameter tuning.
- AI × security became the biggest issue. Anthropic's undisclosed cyberattack model "Mythos," the appearance of OpenAI's "GPT-5.4-Cyber," and concerns over unauthorized access and North Korean abuse, combined with the NIS's abolition of N2SF network separation (effective May), intensified debates over sovereign AI and active defense.
- The decline of SaaS and the rise of the "citizen developer." A trend of non-developers, civil servants (the Ministry of Science and ICT's "AI Sapiens"), and HR staff building agents themselves. KIST switched all research and administrative staff to "self AI" mode and conducted in-house Claude Code training.
- Debate over national AI strategy. Deputy Prime Minister Koo Yun-cheol's remark "SLM, not LLM," the ambiguous goals of independent foundation model (Dokpamo, EXAONE 236B, etc.) initiatives, and heated back-and-forth over the LLM vs. SLM path.

## 🆕 New Releases (Models · Papers · Tools)
- **Claude Opus 4.7** (4/16): Introduced Task Budgets (allocating a token budget across the entire agent loop), strengthened hard prompting, and explicit final execution ordering. Observers noted Sonnet 4.7 was skipped, going straight to 5.0.
- **Anthropic product lineup**: Claude Managed Agents (4/9, automating development, deployment, and management), Claude Design (4/18, an all-in-one design tool based on Opus 4.7, threatening Figma and Canva), Project Glasswing, and a cyber use-case program.
- **OpenAI**: GPT-5.5 (4/24, rated cheaper and better than Opus 4.7), GPT Images 2.0 (4/22, codenamed "Duct Tape," with greatly improved Korean rendering), GPT-5.4-Cyber, and native AWS support (parting ways with Microsoft).
- **Google**: Gemma 4 (4/3, E4b/31b running locally), Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 (spatial and multi-view reasoning), Gemini notebook features + real-time NotebookLM sync, Gemini file generation (Docs/Sheets/Slides, etc.), the Gemini for Mac app, and a partnership with the Korean government (85,000 AlphaFold uses).
- **Open and Chinese models**: DeepSeek V4 (4/24, reportedly trained on Huawei chips), Kimi K2.6 (Moonshot, 1T), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B / Alibaba's Qwen3.6-plus and 3.5-omni shifting to closed-source.
- **Meta**: Muse Spark (4/8, a multimodal reasoning model, criticized for being closed), Muse/MSL, and a vision model for human form and motion.
- **Korean models**: LG EXAONE 4.5 (MoE + VLM, criticized for its restrictive license), and Upstage Solar's presence.
- **Papers**: ModelSpy (estimating DNN architecture at 97.6% from GPU electromagnetic leakage, NDSS), rude prompts yielding better performance (arXiv 2510.04950), Han Dong-soo (KAIST) on AI-human hybrid peer review, Nagoya University's "disappearance of native-language traces in academic papers in the LLM era" (2604.08568), EAP students' intent to disclose AI use (2604.10991), a DeepMind paper on "consciousness/representation learning," and a conversational agent for strengthening grandparent-grandchild relationships (2604.12310).
- **Tools and others**: TurboQuant (KV cache compression, with debate over its real effectiveness), MS MarkItDown (+ hwpx Korean support), the Nemotron Personas Korea dataset, k-skill, Milla Jovovich's mempalace memory system, the Stanford AI Index 2026 (Korea 3rd in notable models, 1st in AI patents per capita, 7th in the dynamism index), Inha University's Ahn Nam-hyuk's public LLM Korean-language lectures, and MIT MathNet.

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- Multi-model cross-validation as a practical standard: a view emerged that having Claude, Codex, and Gemini review simultaneously and synthesizing the commonalities and differences yields greater insight, and that local (qwen3.5) ideas also get adopted.
- The essence of knowledge management is processing, not collecting: while gathering and organizing information has become easy, meaning arises only when you process and connect it through "your own perspective"; otherwise it amounts to "just filling shelves." Karpathy's LLM-wiki × Obsidian workflow was shared as a reference case.
- Debate over incentives vs. penalties in research data sharing: poorly designed incentives mass-produce fake data, so penalties may be better, but the best is a system that gathers data automatically without human hands (autonomous labs). A view emerged that penalties disrupt the supply of new talent and that legislation would be the worst outcome. Meanwhile, a counterargument that penalties are excessive and a view that funnel-type incentives could solve it were also shared. Ultimately there was agreement that voluntary sharing among small groups is the realistic approach.
- The Mythos/security debate: a view emerged that "AI cannot create vulnerabilities that don't exist; what really matters is sovereignty over infrastructure like OS and networks." Meanwhile, a counterargument was raised that "it is a tool that compresses and scales vulnerability discovery, so the threat is real, and the defender must plug every hole while the attacker needs only one." There was also a point that the TCO of an unproven domestic stack is a real-world barrier.
- Workforce and organizations in the agent era: discussion of "3x pay for 10x performers" (citing Shockley) and of cutting labor costs while increasing SG&A. A counterargument held that decision-makers' interest (cutting labor costs) is the basis for predictions and that "only humans bear responsibility."
- AI education, self-efficacy, and workslop: concerns over reduced agency and deepening dependency, with a case of "AI-together" curriculum operation shared. A view emerged that even the $200 plan shows telltale signs of "spineless" workslop. There was agreement that assessment methods should shift toward real-time problem-solving and reasoning.
- Creativity, consciousness, and internal reasoning: Lee Sedol's move 78 was defined as "the process of finding a meaningful uniqueness in a vast combinatorial space," and there was discussion that if vector-space internal reasoning (removing the natural-language prompting bottleneck) is realized, neurosymbolic AI and XAI could be solved at once. A perspective was also shared that "harness engineering is, broadly speaking, one of the neurosymbolic techniques."
- Physical AI and safety: the aerospace demand for "airworthiness certification," deterministic reasoning, and XAI, and the importance of a safety layer as physical AI spreads, were emphasized. There was consensus that with much tacit field knowledge, real-world application is far harder than the paper-level suggests.
- AI misrecognition and human-in-the-loop: the case of a license plate misread as 3→4 leading to a fine (which was actually a company's human error) highlighted the need for verification.
- The AI bubble debate and monetization: 2026 is a transition period moving "from illusion to reality," an era of strictly calculating token value, requiring a clear-eyed view of the technical debt and security risks behind polished-looking outputs. Concerns were raised that the trifecta of inflated expectations, misinterpreted RFPs, and non-expert personnel amid a surge in AI projects will expand implementation issues.
