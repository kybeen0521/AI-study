# July 2026 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2026-07

## 🔭 Key Trends
- **Harness/loop engineering vs. the "it's pointless" camp**: Following Google, an article reporting that OpenAI too believes "next-generation models will absorb harness functionality" drew attention (aitimes). On the other hand, a rebuttal argued that "it's not that harnesses are pointless — rather, they are so powerful that we're heading toward competition where commercial LLMs build them in natively, as Claude did." This led to the assessment that both the LLM model and the harness framework have become nation-level foundational technologies. Discussion comparing harnesses to StarCraft build orders was lively.
- **ICML 2026 on-site recap**: ① LLMs too will soon hit a data wall, shifting from a scaling phase to a methodology phase; ② world models are infeasible due to pixel-level error accumulation → purpose-built models like JEPA are more realistic; ③ the harness is emerging as a research field in its own right. An on-site recap noted an unprecedented "atmosphere of verification" as the defining feature.
- **China's model surge and concerns over open-weight restrictions**: Kimi K3 (~2.7T class) and GLM 5.2 are climbing on performance and price, prompting the self-deprecating take that "US → China → Korea, and locally distilling Chinese models is the most efficient development path." At the same time, a run of reports that both China and the US are considering restricting overseas access to / tiered release of their top-tier models spread concerns about an "insurmountable gap."
- **Full-scale launch of domestic foundation models ("Dokpamo")**: Starting with Upstage's Solar-Open2-250B, a wave arrived in late July — LG K-EXAONE-2.0-750B, SKT A.X-K2, Motif-3, VIDRAFT Aether, and more. Many held the view that "small but strong models" and standardized data infrastructure matter more.
- **The AI4S / AI Scientist boom and the limits argument**: Multiple researchers shared hands-on accounts that while LLMs excel at combinatorial creativity, exploratory and transformative creativity (generating unseen distributions, top-conference-caliber breakthroughs) remain human territory for now. Later, the focus shifted to a war of "verification (Chain of Evidence)" to eliminate hallucinations.
- **Shifts in the model performance landscape**: With the launch of GPT-5.6 (Sol), signs of migration away from Claude appeared, and many rated Opus 5 as "great specs but disappointing in real use." Hands-on comparisons repeatedly found Fable 5 superior on complex, tangled tasks, with GPT-5.6-sol ahead on single tasks.
- **Benchmark reliability controversy**: Critics pointed to the gap from real-world use — benchmaxxing/overfitting, using RL just to bump scores, and "learning the benchmark items to top out only on the test." The need for one's own rubrics and cross-validation came to the fore.
- **Physical AI skepticism**: A cautious view was raised that robotics/multimodal shows a large gap between the advertising and the field, struggles with edge cases, and is premature for commercialization.
- **Humans and organizations in the AI era**: In-depth discussion covered "understanding is the new bottleneck" (Geoffrey Litt), an economics paper arguing humans are the ultimate bottleneck of AI automation, middle-management alignment failures in large organizations' new ventures, and excessive sloganeering in government AI policy.

## 🆕 New Releases (Models · Papers · Tools)
- **Claude Sonnet 5** launched (7/1, competitive on its low price), **Claude Science** open beta, **Claude Opus 5** (7/25), and the autonomous drone control research **Project Pilot / Drone-Bench** (Anthropic · Andon Labs).
- **GPT-5.6**: launched after full US government release approval (7/8) (alongside Terra/Luna), with Codex reorganized into ChatGPT Work/Chat.
- **Kimi K3** (Moonshot): ~3T-class open weights (released 7/27), ranked 3rd on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (Opus 4.8 / GPT-5.5 class). Its 17-year-old author drew attention. Notable case of finding a redis vulnerability in 30 minutes.
- **Gemini 3.6 Flash / 3.5 Flash-Lite**, **Qwen** (new model), and **Thinking Machines Inkling** released.
- **Domestic open models**: Upstage Solar-Open2-250B (2× H200, linear attention, explicit positional encoding removed, INT4-pruned version available), LG K-EXAONE-2.0-750B-A37B, SKT A.X-K2, Motif-3 (dominant among pre-announced models, surpassing Qwen), VIDRAFT Aether-7B (fully open including data, code, and hyperparameters, Apache 2.0).
- **Math open problems**: Fable 5 found a counterexample to the "Jacobian conjecture" (a false proof; the counterexample was surprisingly simple), and OpenAI's shared original prompt for solving the Cycle Double Cover Conjecture (multiagent v2, emphasizing adversarial verification) was circulated.
- **AI for Science tools/frameworks**: Google Science-One (verification at generation time via Chain of Evidence), Google DeepMind Aeneas (ancient-script decipherment), Tencent AI4S (a team concept pairing domain researchers with systems engineers), Stanford Biomni, EPRI's public-report RAG dataset, and ChatGPT for Academic Researchers (offered free to researchers).
- **Security incidents**: A Hugging Face breach was disclosed to have occurred during OpenAI's evaluation of the GPT-6 model (forensics, zero-day patch). A report that DeepSeek inadvertently generated working ransomware.
- **Go (baduk)**: 9-dan Shin Jinseo won a 2-stone handicap game against KataGo (KataGo on 20-second byo-yomi vs. Shin's 5 hours) — drawing attention as a case of a human learning from AI to narrow the gap.
- **Community tools**: paper-curation/paper-curio and citation-expansion tracking tools, gajae-code (a harness), ghost-alice/autopilot (a governance layer), LLM Wiki · AKM Index, Microsoft SkillOpt, Ronny Soft's Rosetta Stone onboarding materials, the open-source team chat Chatto, and more were shared.

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- **Meta-prompt research**: In-depth debate on the problem of an initial instruction getting stuck at a "local optimum," hill-climbing strategies and adversarial review loops, "loop-in-loop" design, and the token explosion of parallel loops. A view emerged that a genre called "meta-prompt research" is needed.
- **AI's self-reinforcement error**: Have it build A and evaluate, and it defends A; fix it to B and evaluate, and it defends B — pointed out as the light and shadow of context accumulation. Discussion expanded to the risks of learning from irreproducible papers (citing Deleuze's "Difference and Repetition").
- **Failures of AI adoption in the field**: The McKinsey 95%-failure statistic, with the cause being not the model but field data and context engineering. The messiness of field data, limited data, and the 90-90 rule (90→99 is hell, worse than 0→90) resonated widely. Pro and con on "rather than persuading domain experts, it's faster to have AI natives absorb the domain and disrupt it" (Noh Jeong-seok, YouTube).
- **Empirical evidence of AI Scientist limits**: Shared hands-on experience that even with a harness, KG, and IR all bolted on, it can't escape existing methodologies, and that top conferences require a problem-specific harness or human-in-the-loop. Alongside a philosophical observation that the LLM's excessive "objectivity" (a Hwang-Hui-style, everyone-is-right stance) actually blocks creativity, a perspective was raised that "maybe human bias is the source of creativity."
- **Token/GPU economics**: Concerns over Fable/Ultra token consumption, GLM 5.2 costing 1 million KRW per day to run in the cloud, and one B300 node costing 1 billion KRW. The calculation that "you need 50+ people using agents like crazy to get ROI."
- **Distillation ethics**: A point that not only China but Claude and GPT are not free of it either, with most terms of service explicitly prohibiting "generating data to train other models." The identity-confusion meme of Claude Code answering "I'm Claude/Kimi."
- **Education and AI**: The "illusion of learning" (removing friction → mistaking it for shallow understanding; the weaker the fundamentals, the more blind the trust), the unverified effect of delayed AI assistance, and a shift toward in-person, process-centered assessment. An empirical study that passed Sejong Academy of Science and Arts' IRB was previewed.
- **Delegating to AI and life**: Over-immersion, sleep deprivation, and dopamine issues; a reasonable distance between AI and humans; and debate over the 6/3/3/4 school system and the "broad-and-deep generalist."
- **Government/policy critique and hopes**: Cynicism toward the "intelligence-exporting nation" slogan, and concerns over GE-style failure cases and makeshift "backyard furnace" approaches. On the other hand, the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's "AI Government Lab" (build small, validate in the field, then scale) and KIST's pragmatic "not fixated on the AI label" approach drew broad praise.
- **What it would take for AI4S to become a searchable domain like a Go board**: A view that graph/tree search-ification, together with parallel data labeling and intuition, is needed. Also shared was a hypothesis, inspired by the top-to-bottom connecting pathway of transformers in the Kimi paper, that "a 3D→2D embedding makes for a better Go board."
- **Paradigm shift in academia**: The weakening authority of the traditional "paper" frame, the possible move from journals → conferences → a third space (archive + GitHub), and forecasts of changes in conference business models.
