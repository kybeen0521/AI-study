# January 2026 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2026-01

## 🔭 Key Trends
- The sovereign foundation model ("독파모") initiative was the biggest issue of the new year. Upstage's Solar-Open-100B claimed to be developed "from scratch," but allegations of derivation from China's GLM (ZhipuAI) surfaced (similarities in architecture and weights, leftover debugging code, missing license). Upstage rebutted with a public verification on YouTube and added ZhipuAI's copyright to its Hugging Face model card, thereby acknowledging the derivation—which spread into a debate over "what exactly is the standard for 'from scratch'?"
- Subsequently, models from all five sovereign-foundation-model teams were released and the Ministry of Science and ICT held its final briefing. Violation of the rule "no training by directly importing open-source weights" was cited as grounds for disqualification, and NAVER and Kakao announced they would not participate in the repechage.
- China's AI advances stood out. DeepSeek released a string of new Transformer architecture papers—mHC (hyper-connection), Engram (Conditional Memory)—raising expectations for v4, and drew praise for circumventing GPU chip constraints through software optimization. Qwen3-Max-Thinking, with frontier-grade performance, switched to a closed (API-only) model.
- Coding and work agent automation effectively became the central topic of the room. Claude Code, OpenCode, oh-my-opencode/oh-my-claudecode, clawdbot (moltbot→openclaw), the Ralph loop, and more circulated rapidly, with many sharing the firsthand sense that "autonomous iterative agents finally work well." Some even argued middleware platforms like n8n and dify have become pointless.
- CES 2026 was covered live. Keynotes from NVIDIA, AMD, and Lenovo; robots such as Hyundai's Atlas and MobED; and Samsung's tri-fold drew attention. Physical AI established itself as this year's keyword.
- Soaring memory (DRAM/NAND) prices and supply shortages came up repeatedly (roughly 7x versus 2025 Q4, with data centers projected to consume 70% of memory in 2026). This was accompanied by shortages of high-spec Macs for running local LLMs.
- The AI for Science / co-scientist (agent-based automation of scientific research) trend spread. Papers heralding an "age of agents" appeared across many fields, and real cases of hypothesis generation and validation were shared.
- AI policy and regulation issues came to the fore: enforcement of the AI Basic Act along with the release of subordinate regulations and transparency-assurance guidelines, the U.S. Department of Defense's shift to "AI wartime speed," DHS's use of generative AI, and more.

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- The "from scratch" debate: opinions split between "if you retrain even a borrowed architecture, it's from scratch" and "the weight of 'entire' gets diluted." Some argued that "architectures and training methods are standardized and data plus engineering are what matter, so copy-pasting code itself isn't a big problem—but the apparent reference to GLM is regrettable," while others noted that "the process of raising and verifying allegations is itself healthy, and rather than the outcome we should reflect on the ambiguity of the task design and evaluation criteria." A technical rebuttal was also shared: weight similarity should be measured with Pearson correlation, not cosine.
- LLM reliability and bias: testing the new year's controversy across several LLMs yielded the observation that "LLMs rely on the initial input and simply endorse the user's claims (sycophancy); only GPT-5.2 pro extended thinking found the flaws." Citing a paper arguing that personalization induces hallucination, some raised a cautionary view that "it shouldn't be used carelessly for counseling." Know-how for prompts that force critical review ("assume this claim is wrong and try to refute it") was also shared.
- On the Nature paper claiming "AI narrows the breadth of science," contrasting views were shared: one that "applying a methodology inevitably narrows the field, which is a normal phenomenon," and another that "there is a paradox where research clusters around topics that are easy to apply AI to, for the sake of convenience." The framing that "AI is strong at solving problems but defining problems is the human domain" drew broad agreement.
- Agentic AI skepticism: an article citing Karpathy's remark that "agents are cognitively lacking" was shared, sparking a debate over their real-world effectiveness. On the other hand, there was a counterargument that "they're already good enough, and it's the expectations of AGI and the singularity that are excessive."
- There was also cynicism toward Physical AI: alongside views that "it's ultimately just repackaged prior work on sim2real/world models" and "a move to keep the bubble alive with a substance-poor keyword," there was cynicism about buzzwords ("daneo") such as 'Physical AI → Proactive AI → ontology/neuro-symbolic'. Meanwhile, the perspective was introduced that in precision domains such as semiconductors and manufacturing, neuro-symbolic approaches (statistical + symbolic) are more effective than purely data-driven ones.
- The sovereign AI debate: the view was put forward that "a domestic foundation model has a national-defense and infrastructure character that can't be judged on economics alone," followed by agreement that "it should be treated like a weapon."
- Data as competitiveness: the view was emphasized that "for research data, quality beats quantity, raw data and autonomous-experiment data should come first, and turning it into AI-Ready Data is key." TDM (copyright) issues and Korea's data handicap were also pointed out.
- Coding workflow and cost realities: Opus 4.5 is the best for complex tasks, but Claude Code burns credits fast, so combining codex/GLM flat-rate APIs and multi-model orchestration has become mainstream. A case of using 1–2 billion tokens per day (10 Max accounts + proxy load balancing) was shared, along with talk of routing, ToS/account-ban risks, and API bill bombs (5 million won in 3 days, 300,000 won in two days).
- Security and reliability warnings: as autonomous agents like openclaw gain control of computers, cases were reported where a prompt injection changed an entire configuration wholesale. Alongside concerns that it's "a gold mine of a market but a Seongsu Bridge-level risk," caution continued about using non-deterministic outputs in critical fields.
- "Results over tools": as new tools and methodologies pour out daily, arguments returning to fundamentals emerged—"efficiency versus adaptation cost," "so what are you actually building?"—along with warnings about lock-in effects. There was also shared awareness of the need to redefine the purpose of education in the AI era from the ground up.
