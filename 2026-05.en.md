# May 2026 · AI study Monthly Digest

> Source: AI study · Period: 2026-05

## 🔭 Key Trends
- **The rise of Harness Engineering and Skills**: The hottest keyword all month. Claude Skill, CLAUDE.md, multi-agent design, and context management came up repeatedly, and a consensus formed that "in the end, handling the harness well is more effective than the model itself." Many related talks/blogs (harness + tacit knowledge, the gnomon repo, etc.) and hands-on materials were shared.
- **Debate over evaluating work by token usage**: A long discussion, prompted by cases at Silicon Valley, Amazon, and Meta, on whether "token volume = performance" is valid. Skeptics argued most of it is waste at the "why was this cost even incurred" level, and some predicted it will ultimately converge on a cost-vs-return, manager-approval model.
- **AI Slop and the asymmetry of verification**: Low-quality generated content is flooding app stores, papers, open source, and content broadly. The core problem identified: "generation gets cheaper while review gets harder — an asymmetry." Expectations that the authority of papers and peer review will decline, with value realigning around tangible results (revenue, startups, GitHub stars, oral presentations).
- **AI4S (AI for Science) and the AI Scientist going practical**: More cases of non-specialists producing better results than specialists using an AI Scientist, alongside questions about the limits of "recombinant creativity" and reproducibility/verification (the HITL bottleneck). KAERI's move to establish the National AI Science Institute (NAIS) drew attention.
- **Sovereign AI and independent models vs. frontier dependence**: The need to secure in-house models gained traction, citing performance degradation of high-density languages such as Korean under quantization, the possibility that US-China AI governance becomes an "AI-era NPT," and the shrinking of open weights (Qwen 3.6 going API-only).
- **Spread of local LLMs and on-premise**: Real-world reviews of local/low-cost models centered on the Qwen family, with demand for local inference rising in backlash to frontier price hikes. Still, a realist view that in-house model development remains a costly minority pursuit.
- **Physical AI / humanoids**: Boston Dynamics Atlas (simulation-based RL), Hyundai's dark factory, Reflex Robotics, and more. Difficulties in sensor reliability, data quality, and positioning differentiation were noted.
- **FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) policies taking off**: Moves by Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic to embed field engineers, and debate over the temperature gap between North American-style AX (tied to private equity and M&A) versus Korean-style SI.
- **Debate over frontier model pricing and perceived performance**: Claude doubled limits across all plans, while Gemini 3.5's launch came with sharply cut limits (rumored to 1/8) and complaints of perceived performance degradation, prompting reactions like "it's just Claude and Codex now."
- **AI sycophancy and AI psychosis**: The problem of LLMs reinforcing user bias and flattering. Cited a Science paper noting that "in situations where a friend would have slapped you on the back, half of them just nodded along."

Praised for improved performance but with the aftertaste that "tokens melt away." An extensive System Card was published (including an AECI self-improvement threshold benchmark).

## 💬 Discussion & Perspectives
- **The "prompting = skill" debate**: Over 23-year-old Liam Price's use of AI to solve a hard math problem (with Terence Tao mentioned), views clashed between "prompting is a genuine skill/ability" and "the real skill is the discernment to judge results and recognize their value." The analogy "born on third base and thinks he hit a triple" also came up.
- **The "creativity = knowledge/memorization" debate**: A view that "you need materials (memorized, embodied knowledge) for combination and creativity to be possible" clashed with a counterargument that "knowledge can actually hold back new thinking," and from an AI4S angle, a perspective was shared on modeling creativity as edge/node discovery in knowledge graphs.
- **Heated debate on distributed learning and agent networks**: In a discussion sparked by Flower SuperGrid, claims such as "OpenClaw's security relies on Telegram" were raised, followed by rebuttals pointing out factual errors. In the end the claim lost credibility and was put to rest (amid an unresolved identity controversy as well).
- **Deeper debate: in-house models vs. RAG/harness**: A view that "open weights + RAG/harness is enough, no need for trillions" clashed with a counterargument that "B2B expectations are always frontier-grade, so in the end it's a fight over model weight class." Technical details were also shared, including LoRA forgetting, GRPO reproducibility, running SFT+RL in parallel, and hands-on reviews of the Muon optimizer.
- **Realigning the value of AI papers and researchers**: A forecast that papers will evolve into "AI-native papers" (split for humans/AI, with stronger reproducibility rules), while whether independent researchers thrive is a toss-up. It was also noted that "anything anyone can do sees its value collapse."
- **The paradox of incentives**: Shared cases of the side effects (the cobra effect) of incentives placed on data collection and AX. Counterarguments that "the people who need the data should collect it" and that "honor and recognition keep a community running."
- **AX and organizational change**: "Real AX is each person building and using what they need themselves"; top-down development by a central department has limits. A view emerged citing GE's DX failure as a case where "you may not know the right answer, but there are wrong ones." Requires leaders' firm conviction and substantive rewards.
- **The HITL bottleneck and verification burden**: Against a flood of AI-generated papers, verification staff are scarce; citing FDA and Brazilian precedents, "Human in the loop is not optional" coexists with calls to remove the bottleneck.
- **Tacit vs. explicit knowledge**: A view that generative AI accelerates the stages after verbalization but cannot cross the pre-verbalization wall of bodily, embodied sense. Discussion of formalizing it via ontologies and digital twins.
- **Education and admissions**: "Rapid knowledge acquisition and test drilling can be replaced by AI" vs. "solid fundamentals and compulsory education matter more." A view treating adults' lag in adapting as a natural phenomenon.
- **Korea's AI strategy**: Prompted by KBS's <Talent War 2> and the like, the point that "the answer is not outside but within us" — and that unless we clearly answer the med-school-admissions-community-style rebuttal that sustainability is doubtful without market size and export competitiveness, change won't come.
